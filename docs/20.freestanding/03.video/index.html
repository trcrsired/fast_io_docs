The History Behind the Problem

This chapter has been about what freestanding C++ cannot do and why. This talk is the history: how the freestanding subset was specified, why its header list stayed unchanged for two decades while the language grew, how the C++23 work finally shipped a usable list, and where the remaining holes came from. It is by Ben Saks, it is from CppCon 2019, and it is the best one-hour account of the subject.

The Talk

What to listen for, in the order the talk raises it:

  • What "freestanding" was supposed to mean. The intent was a C++ you could use for a program with no OS; what the standard delivered was a list of headers that implied one.
  • Why the language did not shrink. The grammar kept exceptions, RTTI and new even where the runtime could not exist (20.1).
  • The scoring of what is missing. The talk enumerates the headers and explains which ones can be made freestanding and which cannot.
  • Why containers will never make it. vector's interface is a heap and an exception; no wording change fixes that (20.1).
